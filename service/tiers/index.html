    Tiered review: service model, harness model, human

    LLM verdicts are made in three tiers. Every hit records who made its current decision in decided_tier:

    tier who how
    service the service model - the LLM the scanner calls (LLM_API_URL, usually a cheap local model) categorises everything: every LLM rule run
    harness the harness model - a capable model in an agent harness (e.g. Claude Code) connected to the MCP server checks what the service model was unsure about and a sample of what it was sure about
    human a reviewer in the UI, REST or MCP decides what the harness model cannot, and anything else

    Precedence: the service model never overwrites a harness or human decision, the harness model never a human one. A harness answer about a hit a human decided meanwhile, or whose service verdict changed, is refused as stale.

    service LLM run  -->  verdicts, decided_tier 'service'
       |
       v  llm_verify job (control worker, after every service run of a branch; no model call)
verify items (llm_work_items.purpose = 'verify', one per hit):
   escalation  every service need_more_info verdict                      (LLM_VERIFY_ESCALATE, default on;
                                                                          LLM_ESCALATION_DAILY_MAX 2000 per rule a day)
   qa          a sample of the confident service verdicts, positive and  (LLM_QA_SAMPLE_RATE 0.05,
               negative, per rule                                         LLM_QA_DAILY_MAX 500 a UTC day)
   reverify    a harness decision replaced by a service re-ask on new content
       |
       v  harness: llm_work_claim(purpose="verify") -> llm_work_submit (applied at once)
confidence high + match / no_match  --> FINAL: status match / no_match, decided_tier 'harness' (field marks too)
confidence low                      --> not applied: investigate (GitLab code, get_object, search_hits, get_hit),
                                        submit again (the item stays leased, at most 3 rounds)
needs_human + question              --> need_more_info, decided_tier 'harness', asked_by_model:
(or MCP request_human)                  review tab "Questions"
       |
       v  quality: llm_quality (agreement per rule) -> llm_mislabeled(source="verification")
          -> llm_prompt_trial (service model, candidate prompt) -> propose_prompt_change -> a human accepts

    Verify items

    After each service run of a branch, the llm_verify job records verify items for the service verdicts of the branch's current snapshot (service tier, no reviewer, current rule version and core prompts, once per verdict: rule_hits.verify_key):

    • escalation - every service need_more_info verdict (LLM_VERIFY_ESCALATE), at most LLM_ESCALATION_DAILY_MAX (2000) per rule and UTC day: a backfill of millions of objects would otherwise turn every unsure verdict into harness work at once. The verdicts over the cap stay service-decided (need_more_info, in the review queue as before) and are eligible again the next day; the verify job counts them as escalation_overflow in its progress and the job_health snapshot sums the day's items and overflow per rule (escalations);
    • qa - a stable hash draw per verdict at LLM_QA_SAMPLE_RATE over the confident verdicts, positive (match / pending_review) and negative (no_match, field rules store their negative field verdicts too), split evenly over the rules and the two classes when the day's cap LLM_QA_DAILY_MAX is reached;
    • reverify - a harness decision the service model replaced because the subject's content changed.

    A verify call is the rule's own system prompt and rule text, the whole object in the same delimited untrusted <object> block as a rule call, the subject (one field or the object), the service verdict in a second delimited <assessment> block (an assessment to check, never an instruction) and an answer format with verdict, confidence (high | low), needs_human, question and investigation (appsec_discovery.ai.rules.build_verify_prompt, VERIFY_PROMPT_VERSION 2).

    The harness model claims items with llm_work_claim(purpose="verify") (POST /llm_work/claim {"purpose": "verify"}) and answers with llm_work_submit:

    • confidence: high with match / no_match is final: the hit gets that status and tier harness (verifier_model, verified_at; mark_fields rules: the field hits too);
    • confidence: low is not applied: the item stays leased to the caller (lease extended to 900 s) for another round after investigating, at most 3 rounds, then needs_human is required;
    • needs_human: true with a question (or MCP request_human(hit_id, question), POST /hits/{id}/request_human for any machine hit) sets need_more_info, tier harness, asked_by_model with the question in the note: the hit appears in the review tab Questions (hits filter asked_by_model=true).

    Every harness answer applied or refused is logged in llm_verifications (service_status / service_verdict / service_model, the harness verdict, sample, outcome final | asked_human | stale). Open verify items expire when the rule version changes.

    Quality

    GET /llm_quality (MCP llm_quality(rule_id=None); Work desk, Pipeline health, the "Health & quality" tab of an LLM rule) reports per rule and overall:

    • agreement of service and harness model on the QA sample (harness confidence high; overall, per service verdict class, last 7 / 30 days; unsure harness answers counted as unsure);
    • escalation_rate (service need_more_info share), escalations_30d decided by the harness or sent to a human;
    • questions_open and oldest_question_s, harness_decisions, backlog of open verify items by sample;
    • needs_improvement: agreement of the last 30 days below LLM_QA_AGREEMENT_MIN (0.9) with at least 30 samples.

    GET /llm_work/stats (MCP llm_work_stats; the Harness work page) sums up the harness work: items by purpose and status, open verify items by sample, leases held by owner, answers of the last 24 hours by owner.

    Improving a prompt: trials and proposals

    1. llm_mislabeled(rule_id, source="human" | "verification") (GET /llm_rules/{id}/mislabeled): the subjects a human or the harness model decided differently from the service model (rule_hits.machine_status).
    2. llm_prompt_trial(rule_id, prompt, subjects=[...] | from_mislabeled=true, include_controls=20, rerun_current=true) (POST /llm_rules/{id}/trials, UI "Try this prompt"): an llm_trial job runs the candidate system prompt with the service model on those subjects plus random controls whose verdict a human or the harness confirmed, and with rerun_current the current prompt on the same objects (a fair A/B). A trial writes no hits, coverage or rule change. Limits: prompt <= 20000 characters, LLM_TRIAL_MAX_SUBJECTS (200, controls included), LLM_TRIAL_DAILY_CALLS (2000 calls of all trials a UTC day; 429 beyond).
    3. llm_prompt_trial_result(trial_id) (GET /prompt_trials/{id}): per subject expected / current / re-run / new verdict; metrics: fixed, still wrong, newly wrong, control regressions, accuracy before -> after, calls, failures. llm_prompt_trials(rule_id) lists a rule's trials.
    4. propose_prompt_change(rule_id, prompt, trial_id, rationale) (POST /llm_rules/{id}/proposals) needs a finished trial of exactly that text (sha256) and changes nothing. prompt_proposals(rule_id, status) lists proposals.
    5. Only a human accepts: the Proposals tab of the LLM rule page (listed on the Work desk: diff, trial metrics and the re-ask cost, GET /prompt_proposals/{id}/reask) or POST /prompt_proposals/{id}/accept | reject. Accepting makes a candidate version of the rule ("Accept as candidate only") or activates it and starts its rollout ("Accept and roll out", activate: true); see LLM rule lifecycle. There is no MCP tool to accept a proposal or activate a version.

    Candidate prompts from agents are stored and shown as data (a diff against the current prompt); the scanner sends them only as the rule's system prompt, the object stays in the delimited untrusted block.

    Human review

    The Review page (Datasec role) is the triage view: tabs Questions (the harness model asked a human), Pending (pending_review), Model unsure (need_more_info not asked by the model), Verified by harness (match / no_match with tier harness), All; an evidence panel (object, fields with comments and samples, the model's reason and verifier, presence in branches / MRs, other hits on the object); keyboard shortcuts j / k move, c / r / i confirm / reject / request info, u reopen, x select for the bulk actions, o open the hit page, ? help. The same decisions: REST POST /hits/{id}/review (confirm | reject | request_info | reopen, with category, severity, note), POST /hits/review_bulk, MCP confirm / reject / request_info / reopen / update_category / review_hits_bulk (up to 50, note required, dry_run first). Decisions record the reviewer and decided_via (rest | mcp) and make the hit's tier human.

    Deciding by filter (tens of thousands of open hits): GET /hits/clusters?by=schema | field_pattern | object_name | rule_group (MCP hits_clusters) groups the open hits into clusters - the same table schema copied into many services, one field name everywhere, one object name, one rule in one GitLab group - with counts, projects, the share in main, the new ones of the last 7 days, the questions inside (answer those first) and sample ids to look at. POST /hits/decide (MCP decide_hits) applies one action to every hit of a filter (cluster, rule_id, group, status, ... as GET /hits): the dry run (default) says how many hits would change and what is kept - human decisions are never overwritten (unless a human asks for it with include_human), the harness model's questions are skipped, the transitions above apply - and the apply needs expected_total equal to that figure (409 when the set changed meanwhile). Every apply is a review_batches row with the previous state of each hit; POST /hits/decide/{batch_id}/undo restores the hits nobody decided again since; the verdict history of each hit carries batch_id. GET /hits/aggregate gives the counts behind the tabs and tiles (by queue, rule, category, project, group, ...) in one call.
